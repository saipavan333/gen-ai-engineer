  With n=5 problems these intervals are far too wide to compare N values: treat this as a demo of the
  mechanism (and of a vote going wrong), not a measured accuracy curve. Real curves need hundreds of problems.
  Look: more samples cost tokens linearly; accuracy gains are bumpy and flatten out. A vote only helps
  when the model is right more often than any single wrong answer. Measure the curve on YOUR task before
  paying for N=7 (or for a reasoning model's long thinking) in production.
